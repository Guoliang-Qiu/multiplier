The general pass replaced brittle hardcoded node 143 and direct OR-with-ZERO buffers with its actual deleted column-4 Booth signal and inherited gate folds. The handoff's zero bit in column 4 remains counted. Algebraically rewrote each Booth row's shared negative correction: step 0 `negative & (one|two) = negative`; steps 1–3 `negative & (one|two) = negative & ~(generated_m & low)`. Removed the unused compensation at column 18 (output bit 18 copies output bit 17) and one low-column input proven inert because of the handoff's special column-3 compressor. Full-stimulus raw-output mismatch count versus immutable handoff: **0/1,050,624** (sample-based equivalence, not a formal proof). Costs and EVM also measured after final general pass. The original parent has better EVM but 33 higher weighted cost and three more slots; final is an eligible parent-relative Pareto tradeoff. Some existing archive records dominate this circuit, but none was borrowed or changed because the assigned handoff and delivery candidate define this local mutation.

Other general-pass trials: native XOR costs 1011.8 before new recode factorization (vs 1000.8), Boolean SOP/POS 1011.8 vs 990.8, alternative NAND XOR equal cost; alternative full-adder pairings equal cost; a MUX_XOR last-row mapping costs 1022.4 before factoring; distributed Booth negative correction costs 1008.8 before factoring. Replacing the two handoff-special column-3 compressors with library reference costs 1019.8 before factoring and fails raw equivalence (17,150 sample mismatches). Small-cone exact synthesis was not available as an installed callable workspace API, and no Cordis plugin was requested; hand-derived identities were validated with complete packed simulation. Tracing both products exposed no cross-product shared raw input signal because the four buses are independent; inherited caching does share common per-product control cones. MUX_XOR cells were retained.

Directional pass: the external stimulus is uneven on the multiplier buses (b negative 74.95%, d negative 25.05%); observed low-column nonconstant activation is roughly 25% at bit 3 and 34–41% at bits 4–6, while the rare bit-10 row is active 1.566%. Removing bit-4 zero (988.8/96 slots) yielded -54.8557 dB; removing reused bit-5 signal (976.8/96) yielded -55.6247 dB; deleting another bit-3 term (988.8/96) yielded -53.5746 dB; removing bit-4 product cell (981/96) yielded -54.8013 dB; deleting bit-5 or bit-6 product slots yielded -55.2645 and -54.8618 dB respectively. The rare bit-10 deletion caused max error 8.8046875 and -47.2484 dB owing to its high weight. Constant rounding at bit 4 (992.8/98) raised RMSE to .33292043 and produced -57.2060 dB, despite reducing raw signed bias; at bit 5 (993.8/98) gave -55.8163 dB. Dropping any higher compensation constant failed badly (-29.57 dB or worse for bits 10, 11, 13, 15). Extending low-bit OR carry to bit 7 cost 999.8/98 before final dead-slot removal and degraded EVM to -56.2211 dB. Thus no directional trial passed the **strict EVM < -57.6 dB** threshold while improving the final general baseline. All trials remain under this experiment directory; neither immutable snapshot was touched. No substitute stimulus, opaque gate replay, or unavailable synthesis plugin was used.
